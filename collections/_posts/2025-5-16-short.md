I am both amazed and confused by large quantities. I have no way to visualize a billion. A trillion. Infinity. Only when I experience the quantity firsthand do I truly “get” it. It’s almost as if I’m testing my human limits when I see: oceans of red ribbons, fortunes and prayers tied to thick, wise trees; plains of promise, locks forged by declarations of love stuck to the steel mesh of bridge railings; and 弥勒佛 represented as a heavenly warrior taller than 3 Stegosauruses stacked on top of another. 

Therefore, when I visited the tallest mountain in 张家界, I couldn’t comprehend its sheer massiveness. From afar, it looked like cute, in a condescending way, green hills created because the pot of Earth was boiling, brimming with lush. Up close, the rough surface of the cliff side scraped my flesh while projecting my screams. How do I make sense of this illusion, paradox, this enigma?

From afar, the mountains are a delicate painting, confined to the limits of your vision, but once you are physically on them, they form the walls and floors of your house. There. Touchable. The esoteric nature of the mountains make me confused. It’s something so large it makes me question scale. But this lack of perception also will tickle my brain and make me smile. Although I may not understand the mountains, I feel freer than anywhere else. Civilization, society, pressures me to understand, to analyze each and every action, emotion, and human. To dig until my hands bleed raw. Picking at my patience and livelihood, my mushed mind for motive. You might think I’d advise you to take a step closer. No. No matter where you are, admiring from afar, or living “it”, stay where the mountain guides you. Enjoy the view.

**1/20/26 Update**:

I recently (a couple months ago) learned that the sublime often presents something so large or powerful it can evoke feelings of fear and awe, such as storms, mountain ranges, or starry night skies. It seems I discovered this firsthand before any Literature class could teach me. The only part I would like to amend is that I think man-made structures can be sublime.
