**Book Review**: YA Chinese urban fantasy

**Before reading reviews**:
WOWOWOWOW. I am so impressed. Typically, I don’t go for fantasy centered around mythology, but I am so happy I read this one. Yee does an amazing job developing the world by revealing the numerous facets of Genie’s life and incorporating them all together. There’s her best friend, crushes at school, family relationships, and college admissions—oh, not to mention the literal Chinese spirituality aspect. As a 16-year old high school senior, the same age as Genie by the way, this book is close to resembling my ideal fantasy. 

Although Genie is an interesting and dynamic MC (we’ll get into that), I prefer smart, gorgeous, rich MCs that allow readers to live vicariously through them, for no particular reason at all. It’s kind of like when you watch a super attractive Youtuber out of morbid curiosity, wondering how someone can be so perfect. At first, I didn’t like Genie’s personality that much, especially when she refused to accept the existence of yaoguai (妖怪). I mean, you just saw the Demon of Confusion turn into black mush with your own two eyes, what is there to be shrieking and fussing about? Whatever, at least that builds more tension with Quentin ;)

She’s too hot-headed in my opinion. Obviously that’s part of the plot, but it just gets annoying, but I get it, I understand people like that exist. Her sense of righteousness is commendable though, and I liked seeing that. Also, Genie doesn’t have a perfect family life. Sure, no one wants to read a book where the MC is perfect, but what about a book where the flaws are more internal. For example, they could have a supportive, healthy family; decent looks; and generally stable personality. However, on the inside they feel disconnected or yearning for something, but a supernatural sci-fi fantasy event happens; everything changes, and they have to overcome new obstacles and manage their powers. 

Yee’s writing style is unparalleled. He incorporates both modern online slang, AP Lit Vocab (“wont = accustomed ”), and traditional American figures of speech. How?!?! I don’t even know man. Maybe it’s because I’m mostly friends with Indian people or because people nowadays speak all gentrified due to social media, but I WISH that I could write like him. Sentence structure and variety, chefs kiss. Also, he is HUMOROUS, so witty and funny and amazing. I then googled his book, and it turns out, he’s also written for the Avatar franchise--of course bro, what should I have expected.

**Rating**: S Tier
